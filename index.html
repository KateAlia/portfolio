The Dozn

Product design · SwiftUI
2026 — shipping
RU interface · EN in progress

Twelve weeks. One main goal, two optional ones. Everything gets harder as you go — and you don't notice it happening.

Why

Habit trackers fail the same way twice over. Too simple and they're checkboxes with no structure. Too heavy and keeping the tracker costs more than the habit did.

People don't quit because they lack willpower. They quit because nothing taught them rhythm.

Phases you don't see

Every subtask carries three phases. Around week five, "five-minute stretch" quietly becomes "fifteen minutes plus fifty skips."

Same task, same ID, same streak. The rules never appear to change, because technically they didn't.

Badges you actually earn

27 of them, each tied to real behaviour — first task before 8am, seven unbroken days, a clean week. They pay out in coach credits, so the AI coach opens up through use rather than a paywall.

Rest doesn't break it

The streak counts days with at least one task done. A day with nothing scheduled leaves it alone. Only missed tasks break it.

Consistency, not obsession. Sunday shouldn't be a punishment.

The bug I keep telling people about

The heat map lit up the wrong day. Data was fine; the last day rendered first. A single .reversed() in a map chain had built the entire calendar mirrored.

Two minutes to fix, and worth remembering: in SwiftUI the order of modifiers changes what data means, not just how it looks.

Grecha

Product design · SwiftUI
2026 — shipping
RU interface · EN in progress

Recipes as structure — not another page buried under ads and SEO filler.

Why

Recipes live scattered. Notes, screenshots, links, a photo of your grandmother's handwriting. Nowhere is a recipe actually structured — ingredients, steps, timing, category.

45 of them were converted from loose HTML into real data models just to have somewhere to start.

Look around first

You can finish onboarding, browse public recipes and see how editing works without an account. Registration only shows up when you want to save something of your own.

Value first. Sign-up second.

Fork, don't overwrite

Global recipes stay untouchable. Editing one hands you your own copy with a link back to the original — so nothing breaks upstream and your version stays yours.

Making SwiftUI misbehave

The background blobs had to show through every screen. NavigationStack sits on an opaque UIKit layer that ordinary modifiers won't clear.

Fixed with a UIViewControllerRepresentable wrapper that walks the controller tree and clears it, plus a custom tab bar replacing the system one. The splash blobs drift on three unsynchronised cycles — no video asset, just Bézier curves and mismatched timing.

Robin Bobin

AI animation · Direction
Ongoing series

A small dog with big responsibilities. Every episode: what happened, then what the dog thinks happened.

The bet

Most AI video right now runs on hype built from hate. A hook, a shock, something engineered to make you angry enough to comment. It spikes for a week and then it's gone, and nothing is left behind.

A character is the opposite bet. You remember a character. You come back for one. But that only works if he's the same dog in every episode — and staying the same is the hard part.

How it's actually made

Director-style prompting loses the character within seconds — different face, different proportions, different dog. What holds: two static keyframes per shot, interpolation between them, every frame controlled.

Slower. Consistent. And storyboarded before a single frame exists — beats, camera, timing, the joke at the end.

Why bother

There's already enough anger going around. These tools are powerful. I'd rather point mine somewhere warmer.
